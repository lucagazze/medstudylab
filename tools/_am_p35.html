Chapter 7 · Pharmacodynamics

Types of Ligands

Agonist

Binds to receptor → Activates response

Antagonist

Binds to receptor → Blocks activation

AGONISTS

Substances that bind to a receptor and trigger a biological response, mimicking endogenous physiological ligands.

  • Full Agonist: Binds and produces the maximum possible response (100% intrinsic efficacy).
  • Partial Agonist: Produces a submaximal response even at 100% receptor occupancy.
  • Inverse Agonist: Binds to receptors with constitutive baseline activity and suppresses it below resting levels.

ANTAGONISTS

Substances that bind to receptors without triggering activation: their sole function is preventing agonists from binding.

Competitive

Competes directly for the primary agonist binding site. It is reversible: increasing agonist concentration overcomes the block.

Non-Competitive

Binds to an allosteric site or forms an irreversible covalent bond. Changes receptor shape; cannot be overcome by higher agonist doses.
